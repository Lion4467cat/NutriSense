# Research: fiducial food datasets and calorie-app methods

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Purpose:** identify food datasets that can inform NutriSense's reference-card workflow, and summarize what is publicly documented about commercial photo-based calorie trackers and their training/evidence.  
**Scope note:** this is a targeted source review, not an exhaustive search. App behavior and dataset availability can change; check the linked source before acquiring or relying on a dataset.

## Findings at a glance

- **MetaFood3D** is the clearest public research lead that explicitly includes fiducial-marker images in a food 3D/nutrition dataset. Access is request-gated, and its project page limits use to non-commercial research. It is useful to investigate for marker pose/scale and reconstruction methods, but does not replace local Indian weighed captures.
- **SimpleFood45** is a small, measured portion dataset captured with a smartphone and a physical reference. It provides real-food images and ground-truth volume, mass, and energy; it is not described as an ArUco dataset, and its terms/access need checking before use.
- **ECUSTFD** includes 25 mm coin calibration in top/side food photographs plus measured mass and volume. The Hugging Face converted mirror says CC BY 4.0; the upstream project's licensing is not clearly stated in the repo's earlier audit. A coin is a scale reference, not a pose-calibrated fiducial.
- **Nutrition5k** has excellent measured ingredient-weight/nutrition labels and RGB-D, but no reference marker in the capture protocol. It is best used to imitate weighing/annotation and as non-Indian transfer data, not as marker training data.
- Commercial product disclosures are uneven. MyFitnessPal describes image recognition followed by suggestions matched to a food database, with users choosing foods and adjusting servings. Calorie Mama exposes candidate foods, standard serving weights, and database nutrition. SnapCalorie describes depth-sensor volume estimation and a curated weighed-dish dataset. Foodvisor publicly describes photo nutrition logging and grants itself rights to use uploaded photos, but does not disclose a reproducible training corpus/model recipe in its user-facing materials reviewed here.
- Do not treat product claims or recognition as proof of reliable portion mass. Independent studies have found substantial error and/or omitted components, and older platform evaluations found portion estimation unavailable in the tested products. Evaluation conditions and app versions differ.

### Practical collection implication

For a NutriSense dataset, retain both the **reference-card signal** and independent ground truth: full-resolution original image/EXIF; marker ID and detected corner coordinates/pose; card measured dimension; segmentation/component ID; net gram weight per vessel/item (and total); dish/ingredient identity; recipe or nutrition source; capture session, camera, view, and lighting. Take repeated views without moving the plate, then repeat after a controlled pose change. This lets the same data answer marker stability, vessel detection, portion mass, and per-component nutrition questions. The existing [field manual](../data-collection-manual.md) already specifies a 60 mm ArUco card and whole-tray mass; extend its metadata schema for per-item weights and repeat-view IDs rather than mixing these labels into a single total.

### What an openly documented training pipeline looks like

Nutrition5k is a research benchmark rather than a commercial app, but it provides a concrete example of the supervision needed to train this task. During capture, ingredients were added one at a time to a plate and weighed incrementally; nutrition targets were computed from ingredient gram weights and USDA nutrient densities. The released labels include total grams, calories, and macros, while images include overhead RGB-D and rotating side-view video. The paper samples every fifth video frame for RGB-only training and trains shared InceptionV2 features with multitask regression heads for mass, calories, protein, carbohydrate, and fat, optimizing mean absolute error. It also compares portion-normalized nutrient-density prediction and direct whole-meal regression, and evaluates depth-aware models on dishes with depth captures. The published training split keeps all incremental scans of a single plate together to avoid train/test leakage. See the [dataset README](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/Nutrition5k) and [CVPR 2021 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/html/Thames_Nutrition5k_Towards_Automatic_Nutritional_Understanding_of_Generic_Food_CVPR_2021_paper.html).

### Cross-app interpretation

These products generally combine one or more of: (1) food/dish recognition or ingredient proposals; (2) a nutrition database or recipe mapping; (3) some quantity/serving assumption or depth/volume estimate; and (4) user confirmation/editing. A calorie number is therefore not necessarily a direct visual regression target. If identity is wrong, the database mapping is wrong; if portion is wrong, the nutrient total scales with it; hidden oil, sugar, and recipe variation may remain invisible. Treat these as separate model/evaluation stages.

For practical benchmarking, record the app/version, exact capture conditions, suggested food labels, serving quantities, user edits, final nutrient output, and weighed truth. Preserve raw initial suggestions before user corrections. Compare label agreement, per-item grams, per-meal kcal/macros, omissions/false additions, and repeatability separately.

## 3. Independent evaluations and caveats

- A peer-reviewed comparison of commercial food-image platforms reported that none of the tested services could estimate the respective food/beverage portions in that study; the authors explicitly noted that proprietary training methods were unknown. This was an older study and product versions have since changed, so it is evidence of a historical limitation, not a current product ranking: [Use of Different Food Image Recognition Platforms in Dietary Assessment (2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7752530/).
- A 2024 app comparison reported high enough food-identification performance for some apps, including MyFitnessPal and Foodvisor, while finding material energy discrepancies, especially for mixed dishes and missed components: [Evaluating the Quality and Comparative Validity of Manual Food Logging and AI-Enabled Food Image Recognition in Apps for Nutrition Care](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/16/15/2573). It evaluated app versions and study foods of that period; do not project scores onto present releases.
- A CALO mama validation study describes a standard 215-item food list and a deep-learning system predicting food items, ingredients, and portion size, then evaluates nutrient/food-group estimates: [JMIR 2022 validation](https://formative.jmir.org/2022/1/e31875). This is a study description of that app/system version, not a current technical training disclosure.
- Vendor accuracy figures (including SnapCalorie's self-reported mean calorie error) are not directly comparable unless reference meals, capture conditions, aggregation, and ground truth align. Prefer independent, preregistered weighed-meal evaluation; NutriSense's own field data should use plate/dish-held-out splits and report component as well as meal-level errors.

## 4. Recommended next research/data actions

1. Request MetaFood3D access and ask specifically whether marker IDs, corner/pose annotations, per-instance weights, food-code nutrition labels, and redistribution/training rights are included.
2. Confirm SimpleFood45 and ECUSTFD terms and obtain the annotation schema. Use them to prototype known-scale geometry only if permitted; keep their performance claims separate from Indian mixed-meal evaluation.
3. Keep Nutrition5k for weighing-protocol/transfer work and WED for Indian measured-food validation. The marker-specific model should be validated on NutriSense captures with the exact 60 mm ArUco card.
4. If app behavior itself matters, conduct a versioned black-box comparison on a small weighed set with raw predictions and edits logged; public materials do not reveal enough training details to reconstruct proprietary systems.
