\begin{abstract}
NutriSense is an uncertainty-aware web prototype for multi-item food mass estimation and compliance monitoring against school-meal nutritional standards. Plated meals present severe perspective distortion, soft overlapping food boundaries, and occluded secondary vessels. The system couples a 60\,mm ArUco scale reference with a dual-boundary Michelson contrast gate ($C_M \ge 0.42$), DeepLabV3 semantic segmentation across 42 institutional food classes, SigLIP-2 crop classification with exterior context attenuation ($\alpha = 0.65$), a DINOv2-S weight regression head, and Monte Carlo nutrient propagation against PM POSHAN guidelines. On the Weight Estimation Dataset (WED), the semantic segmenter achieves 0.8211 Macro Food IoU and 0.7541 Region F1; the weight head obtains 16.422\,g 5-fold outer cross-validation MAE across 198 plates (11,759 regions) and 15.034\,g DEV MAE. On a held-out benchmark of 23 real-plate smartphone captures, precision fusion yields 29.70\,g MAE (9.42\% MAPE) on primary overhead views with zero duplicate inflation across all views. To scale institutional training, a mass-conditioned appearance generator and real-patch CutMix engine synthesize provenance-audited training data with zero holdout leakage. NutriSense provides a traceable, safety-gated platform for mid-day meal monitoring.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Monitoring institutional mid-day meal programs requires accurate tracking of cooked portion mass and macro-nutritional content (energy and protein) against statutory standards. In India, the Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman (PM POSHAN) scheme specifies mandatory daily calorie and protein minimums for primary (classes I--V: 450\,kcal, 12\,g protein) and upper-primary children (classes VI--VIII: 700\,kcal, 20\,g protein) \cite{pmposhan2022guidelines}. Manual weighing of plates disrupts cafeteria flow and imposes prohibitive operational overhead. Camera-based optical estimation offers a frictionless alternative, yet single-image institutional captures present severe computer vision challenges: uncalibrated perspective tilt, specular reflections from stainless steel thalis, soft blending between adjacent curries, and obscured side bowls (katoris).

NutriSense addresses these challenges via a staged, safety-first estimation pipeline. The system enforces strict physical grounding through a dual-boundary fiducial marker, extracts disjoint semantic regions via DeepLabV3, suppresses dominant plate context during SigLIP-2 crop classification, and fuses metric volume integration with a learned DINOv2-S mass head via inverse-variance weighting. All nutritional inferences are propagated probabilistically, withholding compliance verdicts when evidence confidence falls below safety thresholds.

\section{Related Work}
Fiducial markers enable metric scale recovery from single view RGB images under perspective distortion \cite{garrido2014aruco}. While classical ArUco implementations rely on simple border contrast, kitchen environments with specular stainless steel surfaces require robust quiet-zone verification to eliminate false card admissions.

For food segmentation, promptable models such as SAM 2 \cite{ravi2025sam2,meta2024sam2repo} provide powerful zero-shot masks but lack semantic class awareness, often over-segmenting continuous rice mounds or splitting liquid curries. Task-specific semantic architectures such as DeepLabV3 trained on institutional thali benchmarks \cite{arun2025thali} provide superior class-aware regional consistency.

In dish recognition, large vision-language models such as SigLIP-2 \cite{tschannen2025siglip2} achieve strong zero-shot and few-shot representation. However, when applied to tightly cropped food regions on a shared platter, ambient rice and background clutter leak into the bounding box, distorting feature embeddings. Metric depth estimation from single images has advanced via models like MoGe-2 \cite{wang2025moge2}, which produce dense affine-invariant depth maps that can be scaled using a visible metric reference. For mass estimation, learned representations leveraging frozen foundation features such as DINOv2 \cite{oquab2024dinov2} provide direct mass regression from visual texture.

\section{System Architecture}
Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline_flow} illustrates the end-to-end execution flow. When a meal photograph is submitted, input validation gates first confirm minimum resolution ($\ge 3000$\,px along the long axis), valid weekday metadata, and supported PM POSHAN class bands.

The scale-anchor stage detects the 60\,mm ArUco marker. A dual-boundary Michelson contrast test validates the marker's quiet zone before extracting metric pixel scale ($s_{xy}$) and normal vector $\mathbf{n}$. If marker detection fails, the pipeline transitions gracefully to an uncalibrated prior tier, documenting the assumption in the wire contract.

Food regions are segmented using DeepLabV3 with a ResNet-50 backbone. For complex multi-item platters, secondary vessels (katoris) are isolated using physical area and solidity criteria. Contested boundary pixels between adjacent food items are partitioned deterministically using distance transforms to exclusive region cores.

Each isolated region is classified by SigLIP-2 against a 41-class gallery. An exterior attenuation mask dampens background platter pixels, ensuring that small side curries are not misclassified as the surrounding rice mound. Portion masses are computed by fusing metric geometric integration with a class-conditional DINOv2-S weight head via inverse-variance weighting.

Finally, Monte Carlo sampling ($n=4000$) propagates measurement uncertainty through cooked-dish recipe composition tables, calculating energy (kcal) and protein (g) distributions. A strict compliance engine evaluates these distributions against statutory thresholds.

\section{Algorithmic Innovations and Formulations}

\subsection{Dual-Boundary Michelson Quiet-Zone Gate}
Specular glares on stainless steel plates frequently generate false-positive ArUco candidate quads. To guarantee marker integrity without manual thresholds, NutriSense samples two concentric contours around the marker: an outer white quiet-zone contour at $+0.35$ module widths and an inner black perimeter contour at $-0.50$ module widths. The Michelson contrast $C_M$ is computed as:
\begin{equation}
C_M = \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{white}} - \mu_{\mathrm{black}}}{\mu_{\mathrm{white}} + \mu_{\mathrm{black}}},
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{\mathrm{white}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{black}}$ denote the mean luminance of the outer and inner contours, respectively. Markers are admitted if and only if $C_M \ge 0.42$. Across our 23-view benchmark, this criterion achieved 100\% admission of valid markers with zero false admissions.

\subsection{3D Tabletop Base Plane Unprojection}
In handheld captures, calculating food height from raw depth values produces severe underestimation if the plate rim truncates the food base plane. NutriSense unprojects the four ArUco quad corners into 3D metric camera space using the intrinsic camera calibration matrix $K$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_i = Z_i \cdot K^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} u_i \\ v_i \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}.
\end{equation}
The true tabletop support plane $\mathbf{n}^T \mathbf{p} + d = 0$ is estimated via singular value decomposition over the unprojected marker corners. For any food pixel $(u, v)$ with estimated metric depth $Z$, its height $h(u, v)$ above the tabletop is the orthogonal distance to the table plane:
\begin{equation}
h(u, v) = \mathbf{n}^T \left( Z \cdot K^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right) + d - h_{\mathrm{floor}},
\end{equation}
where $h_{\mathrm{floor}}$ is clamped to $h_{\mathrm{floor}} \le 0.35 \cdot h_{\mathrm{median}}$ to prevent the thali rim from cutting off shallow food layers.

\subsection{Context-Attenuated Crop Classification}
When classifying a crop $I_{\mathrm{crop}}$ extracted from bounding box $B_i$, background food items inside $B_i$ outside the target mask $M_i$ bias the SigLIP-2 image encoder. NutriSense applies soft exterior attenuation with factor $\alpha = 0.65$:
\begin{equation}
I_{\mathrm{attenuated}}(\mathbf{x}) = I_{\mathrm{crop}}(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \left[ M_i(\mathbf{x}) + \alpha \left(1 - M_i(\mathbf{x})\right) \right].
\end{equation}
This retains boundary context while reducing background gradient magnitude by 35\%, preventing false condiment classifications on secondary liquid vessels.

\subsection{Secondary Vessel Geometric Containment}
Liquid dishes served in small metallic katoris placed directly on the thali platter are constrained by physical geometry:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
0.01 \le \frac{\mathrm{Area}_{\mathrm{item}}}{\mathrm{Area}_{\mathrm{plate}}} \le 0.12, \quad \frac{w}{h} \ge 0.50, \\
\frac{\mathrm{Area}_{\mathrm{item}}}{\mathrm{Area}_{\mathrm{convex}}} \ge 0.60.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Proposals that violate these bounds or contact the plate boundary are disqualified, eliminating rim glare artifacts. Overlapping items belonging to the same culinary family (e.g., steamed rice fragments) are coalesced via union $M_{\mathrm{merged}} = M_1 \cup M_2$.

\subsection{Inverse-Variance Precision Fusion}
For food items with both metric geometry and learned representations, mass estimates are blended using their respective empirical error variances:
\begin{equation}
\hat{m} = \frac{w_{\mathrm{geom}} \hat{m}_{\mathrm{geom}} + w_{\mathrm{learned}} \hat{m}_{\mathrm{learned}}}{w_{\mathrm{geom}} + w_{\mathrm{learned}}},
\end{equation}
where $w_{\mathrm{geom}} = 1/\sigma_{\mathrm{geom}}^2$ and $w_{\mathrm{learned}} = 1/(2.20 \cdot \sigma_{\mathrm{learned}})^2$. The scaling factor 2.20 reflects the higher empirical variance of the uncalibrated learned prior under variable smartphone camera ISP pipelines.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}

\subsection{Stage-Wise Component Verification}
Table~\ref{tab:stage-metrics} presents empirical performance across individual pipeline stages.
The DeepLabV3 model achieves 0.8211 Macro Food IoU across 38 represented DEV classes and 0.9196 Foreground Union IoU on the 108 DEV views (21 plates). Compared to class-agnostic SAM 2.1 dense-grid proposals, DeepLabV3 achieves a $3.6\times$ higher Region F1 (0.7541 vs.\ 0.2067) while reducing spurious non-overlapping proposals from 857 to 61.

For mass regression, the DINOv2-S weight head was evaluated via a 5-fold outer cross-validation protocol over 198 WED TRAIN plates (11,759 ROIs), excluding the 21 DEV plates and the locked test set. It achieved a weighted outer MAE of 16.422\,g ($\pm 0.884$\,g fold standard deviation). On the held-out DEV carve, it achieved 15.034\,g MAE; on the frozen WED LOCKED split (2,793 ROIs), it achieved 14.667\,g MAE with 82.77\% nominal 90\% coverage.

\subsection{End-to-End Real Plated Meal Benchmark}
The full multi-item pipeline was benchmarked on 23 smartphone captures spanning six distinct plated meals weighed on a calibrated physical balance (\texttt{Aruco\_7ID\_60mm\_with\_food}). Table~\ref{tab:e2e-metrics} summarizes results.
On primary overhead captures, NutriSense achieved an MAE of 29.70\,g (9.42\% MAPE). Individual plate errors included Dish 5 (+0.8\%, 151.2\,g vs.\ 150\,g), Dish 3 ($-2.9\%$, 185.4\,g vs.\ 191\,g), and Dish 2 (+8.6\%, 367.1\,g vs.\ 338\,g). On extreme perspective tilts ($\ge 50^\circ$), self-occlusion of katoris increased pooled MAE to 122.55\,g. Crucially, duplicate proposal coalescence and decoy suppression eliminated all phantom papad/salad detections, reducing item-to-plate mask area ratio from $7.71\times$ to $1.00\times$.

\subsection{Provenance-Audited Synthetic Augmentation}
To address institutional data scarcity, an 83-channel mass-conditioned appearance generator (\texttt{semantic\_unet\_83ch\_mass\_conditioned\_v2}: 42 semantic channels + 41 log-mass channels) and real-patch CutMix engine were deployed. The pipeline generated 82 composite plates. An automated cryptographic audit verified zero data leakage: 0\% overlap with WED DEV, WED LOCKED, or ArUco benchmark images. Under preregistered model promotion guardrails (paired 10,000-replicate group-bootstrap 95\% CI upper bound $< 0$), the frozen baseline model (16.422\,g outer CV) is retained for deployment, while the synthetic corpus is cataloged for multi-task pre-training.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
While NutriSense delivers sub-10\% portion error on overhead views, physical sensing limitations remain:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Submerged Solids:} Monocular surface RGB cannot determine vegetable-to-broth ratios inside opaque sambar or rasam, necessitating recipe prior assumptions.
\item \textbf{Grain Packing Density:} Uncompressed fluffy basmati rice ($0.70\text{ g/cm}^3$) versus dense compacted rice ($0.95\text{ g/cm}^3$) introduces irreducible $\pm 15\%$ volumetric density variance.
\item \textbf{Steep Perspective Views:} Views exceeding $50^\circ$ tilt obscure food behind vessel walls, where monocular depth cannot reconstruct the occluded base.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
NutriSense demonstrates an auditable, uncertainty-aware framework for automated school-meal nutritional monitoring. By unifying robust fiducial anchoring, semantic segmentation, context-attenuated recognition, and precision portion fusion, the system eliminates duplicate item inflation and delivers sub-10\% MAPE on primary meal views. All code, wire contracts, and evaluation manifests are fully open and reproducible.
