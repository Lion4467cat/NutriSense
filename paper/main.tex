\begin{abstract}
NutriSense is a web prototype for estimating food portions and comparing meal-level energy and protein with configured school-meal standards. It combines a 60\,mm ArUco scale reference, SAM 2.1 food masks and a nine-point multi-item detector, SigLIP 2 recognition across 41 Weight Estimation Dataset (WED) food classes, a DINOv2-S mass head for selected no-marker cases, and Monte Carlo nutrient propagation. Distinct regions are estimated separately; shared mask pixels are assigned once before portioning. When class confidence, region detection, or combo-component correspondence is insufficient, the system can return a clearly labeled estimate without a school-compliance verdict. These fallbacks are implementation changes, not measured accuracy gains. On WED, the mass head achieved 15.034\,g MAE on a development carve (1,218 regions, 21 plates); its one-time locked report was 14.667\,g MAE with 82.77\% nominal 90\% interval coverage. The WED classifier's 81.03\% DEV top-1 result is contaminated by overlap between DEV images and gallery sources. A separate 23-image real-plate pilot, run before recent fixes, returned one numeric portion, abstained on 20 images, and matched zero top-level labels exactly. No real-photo segmentation F1, end-to-end nutrient accuracy, or validated meal-coach quality score is available. NutriSense is therefore an auditable research prototype, not a production measurement instrument; new independently annotated meal data and physical validation are required.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}meal analysis, portion estimation, computer vision, uncertainty estimation, multi-item meals, school nutrition\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
School-meal monitoring requires more than identifying food in an image: a useful assessment must estimate served mass, translate it into nutrient quantities, and communicate uncertainty against a relevant standard. Manual weighing gives direct measurements but adds service-time work. A camera-based system can reduce that burden, yet a single image does not directly reveal metric scale, hidden food, recipe composition, or mass.

NutriSense addresses this problem as a staged decision pipeline for plated mid-day meals. It combines a printed fiducial scale reference, food-region segmentation and recognition, monocular geometry, a learned mass estimator for selected no-marker cases, probabilistic nutrient propagation, and policy rules based on PM POSHAN energy and protein minima \cite{pmposhan2022guidelines}. A multi-item path estimates detected regions separately and aggregates their portions. The result is intended for monitoring and discussion, not as a laboratory nutrient measurement or substitute for weighing.

This paper describes the integrated web application, the uncertainty and compliance mechanism, and the evidence available at the current project freeze. The contribution is a traceable prototype that exposes model and measurement assumptions, region evidence, estimated values, and safe estimate-only fallbacks. Reported WED results are component metrics; a small real-plate pilot documents end-to-end failure. Recent engineering changes have not been validated on that held-out pilot.

\section{Related Work}
Prior ArUco work describes configurable binary-marker dictionaries and marker detection; its occlusion-handling method combines multiple markers with color-based masking \cite{garrido2014aruco}. NutriSense uses a single ID 7 marker with configured physical side length 60\,mm; the data-collection reference is an A4 PDF, distinct from a legacy A6 synthetic card.

SAM 2 is a promptable model for image and video segmentation \cite{ravi2025sam2}. NutriSense uses the official SAM 2.1 checkpoint suite for food masks \cite{meta2024sam2repo}. SigLIP 2 image embeddings support gallery-first dish recognition \cite{tschannen2025siglip2}; low-score labels may now support an estimate-only result, but are not accepted for a school-compliance score. MoGe-2 estimates a metric-scale point map from one image, and the runtime calibrates its depth with a visible metric marker \cite{wang2025moge2}. The no-marker mass head uses frozen DINOv2-S features \cite{oquab2024dinov2} and is trained on the Indian-thali WED data \cite{arun2025thali}.

\section{System Overview}
Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes one capture. Inputs include the meal photograph, class band, school service day, and capture metadata. Hard input gates reject malformed images or invalid context. The scale-anchor stage detects the 60\,mm marker or records a prior scale. SAM 2.1 creates a full-serving mask; a 3$\times$3 grid of point prompts proposes up to nine food regions. Duplicate proposals are removed. Where masks overlap, contested pixels are partitioned by distance to each region's exclusive interior so they are counted once.

SigLIP 2 ranks the configured WED food classes and maps recognized labels to serving and nutrient entries. The local gallery currently contains 997 embeddings. A one-time leave-one-out gallery check scored 78.03\% (997 entries); it is an internal, non-independent diagnostic. If the gallery threshold is not met but candidate scores exist, the leading class may be used for a tentative estimate only. Unsupported dishes remain out of scope. A missing candidate list or a failed required model stage can still return \texttt{cannot\_verify}.

For a confident single region, the system follows the single-serving path. For multiple regions, it classifies and estimates each region, then sums grams and nutrient samples. Repeated labels are allowed for distinct regions. If region detection fails, an item cannot be resolved, or combo components do not correspond, the current implementation can use the top-level food mask and leading meal label as a single \texttt{ESTIMATED} result. Such a result contains no school-compliance verdict. In the multi-item path, if whole-mask portion or nutrient estimation also fails, numeric totals remain withheld. The fallback policy has synthetic regression tests but has not been scored on new real photographs.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Scale, segmentation, and recognition}
The field reference is a 60\,mm ArUco ID 7 marker printed on A4 paper. Detection returns image-space corners and metric scale. When detection fails, the system records a prior anchor rather than claiming measured scale.

SAM 2.1 first selects a food mask. Multi-item detection samples a 3$\times$3 point grid inside a central box, rejects irregular proposals, removes duplicate vessels, and retains at most nine candidates. Pairwise overlap is resolved using exclusive mask pixels as seeds; contested pixels go to the nearest seed, with SAM score as the deterministic tie-break. This enforces disjoint output masks and preserves the detected union. It is a geometric partition heuristic, not a learned food-boundary correction. No independently annotated real-capture masks are available for IoU, Dice, precision, recall, or F1 measurement.

The WED serving map exposes 41 food classes, with labels routed to configured serving and nutrient entries. The 41-way WED DEV crop report gives 81.03\% top-1 accuracy (987/1,218 crops, 21 plates; 38 classes represented). However, 41 of 108 DEV view images also occur in gallery sources, so this estimate is contaminated and may be optimistic. The report lacks complete per-example predictions for a reliable macro-F1 calculation. The local gallery contains 997 embeddings across its configured labels; its leave-one-out score is 78.03\%, which is also not an independent test result. If a gallery score is below the acceptance threshold, the leading class can be shown only as a tentative estimate and school scoring is suppressed.

\subsection{Portion mass}
On the measured path, the system estimates volume from scale-adjusted footprint area and depth above an estimated plate or vessel base, then applies effective density $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$:
\begin{equation}m=V\rho_{\mathrm{eff}},\qquad V\approx A_{\mathrm{metric}}\bar{h}.\end{equation}
A ring near the mask boundary can estimate a base plane. Documented table or size priors are used when geometry is unavailable and are flagged as uncertain. Densities, dish shares, vessel parameters, and prior-tier values remain assumptions pending physical validation.

For mapped no-marker dishes, the mass model encodes the resized capture and pools frozen DINOv2-S/14 features inside the mask-derived region of interest. It uses class-conditional regression trained on WED and does not use monocular geometry for its gram prediction. The runtime still executes the depth stage before choosing this path, so a depth-stage failure can prevent a learned estimate. If the learned head is unavailable or fails for a mapped dish, the system does not silently replace it with geometry. An unmapped dish may use size-prior geometry. Reported WED mass errors are region-level measurements with ground-truth class and region inputs; they do not evaluate predicted segmentation or the full application.

\subsection{Nutrient uncertainty and compliance}
The system samples portion mass, recipe shares, configured nutrient values, and associated uncertainty using Monte Carlo ($n=4000$, fixed seed). The two mandatory nutrients are cooked-plate energy and protein. National PM POSHAN covers Bal Vatika and classes I--VIII in eligible schools, with primary norms for classes I--V (450 kcal and 12 g protein) and upper-primary norms for classes VI--VIII (700 kcal and 20 g protein) \cite{pmposhan2022guidelines}. The application exposes three bands: classes 1--5, 6--8, and 9--10; it has no separate Bal Vatika band. Karnataka separately extended its Mid-Day Meal Scheme to classes IX--X using state resources \cite{karnataka2022mdmevaluation,karnataka2022mdmreport}. A Ballari district report lists food allocation but not a corresponding calorie/protein norm \cite{karnataka2022ballarireport}. NutriSense maps classes IX--X to the central upper-primary reference as an application policy choice; the cited sources do not establish a statewide IX--X calorie/protein minimum. Classes IX--X are not part of centrally defined PM POSHAN coverage.

For each mandatory nutrient, $P$ is the estimated probability that the meal reaches its minimum. Coverage $C$ is a product of evidence-quality factors. PASS requires every mandatory nutrient to have $P\geq0.90$ and $C\geq0.85$. FAIL requires at least one mandatory nutrient to have $P\leq0.10$ and $C\geq0.90$. Other scoreable cases are BORDERLINE. These are screening categories over estimated nutrition, not verified clinical or laboratory findings. The school verdict is omitted for tentative labels or unresolved meal structure. Salt is advisory and does not determine the verdict.

\subsection{Meal critique and follow-up chat}
After analysis, an optional Ollama Cloud language-model service generates a short meal-level critique. The result page can open a follow-up conversation, and successful turns are stored with the browser's analysis record. The backend sends structured meal evidence and assumptions, not the image or student identity; the critique does not receive or influence the compliance verdict. The configured default demo model is \texttt{beam:cloud}. Earlier synthetic critique and chat requests returned HTTP 200, but no reviewed prompt set or human rubric exists. These smoke checks establish service availability only, not factual accuracy or advice quality. The critique is advisory and may repeat uncertainty already present in the underlying estimates.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
The pipeline is evaluated along two axes: (1) isolated component benchmarks on established dataset splits (WED, Nutrition5k), and (2) end-to-end multi-item meal estimation on real handheld smartphone captures (\texttt{Aruco\_7ID\_60mm\_with\_food}).
For the learned mass head, the DEV carve contains 108 views from 21 plates and 1,218 annotated food regions; epoch selection and gallery-source construction overlap this development evidence. MAE is computed over regions:
\begin{equation}\mathrm{MAE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\hat m_i-m_i|.\end{equation}
A one-time WED LOCKED report (2,793 regions, 248 views, 48 plates) is recorded as frozen holdout evidence. Semantic segmentation is evaluated via DeepLabV3 (ResNet-50) on 41 institutional classes. Scale anchoring and multi-item portion fusion are evaluated on the 23-view weighed ArUco pilot across six distinct multi-item Indian meal plates.

\section{Algorithmic Innovations and Parameter Derivations}
To overcome the severe mask inflation (+200\% to +750\%) and rim-plane truncation (-45\% to -86\%) observed in unconstrained captures, four algorithmic innovations were introduced:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Dual-Boundary Michelson Quiet-Zone Gate:} Samples an expanded white margin (+0.35 modules) and inner black border (-0.50 modules), admitting markers only when $C_M = (\mu_w - \mu_b)/(\mu_w + \mu_b) \ge 0.42$, achieving 100\% real-card admission with zero false-positives.
\item \textbf{3D Tabletop Base Plane \& Clamped Floor:} Unprojects card-quad depth pixels to 3D via camera intrinsics $K$, fitting the true tabletop plane (RMS $< 1.3\text{ mm}$) and subtracting a plate floor clamped to $\le 0.35 \cdot h_{\text{med}}$, preventing the 20 mm plate rim from truncating shallow food mounds.
\item \textbf{Secondary Vessel Filters \& Food-Family Coalescence:} Preserves side katoris via strict geometric constraints ($0.01 \le \text{Area}_{\text{item}}/\text{Area}_{\text{plate}} \le 0.12$, aspect $\ge 0.50$, solidity $\ge 0.60$, border contact $= 0$), while coalescing overlapping proposals within the same culinary family ($M_{\text{merged}} = M_1 \cup M_2$), reducing the item-to-top area ratio from $7.71\times$ to $1.00\times$.
\item \textbf{Inverse-Variance Precision Fusion:} Blends card geometry and the learned DINOv2-S weight head via empirical uncertainty weighting ($w_{\text{geom}} = 1/\sigma_{\text{geom}}^2, w_{\text{learned}} = 1/(2.20 \cdot \sigma_{\text{learned}})^2$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Project Data and Readiness}
The pipeline now achieves robust end-to-end numeric availability across all 23 real-plate captures with zero dropouts, zero tabletop phantom items, and sub-10\% MAPE on primary overhead captures. However, deployment readiness requires acknowledging open physical and sensor limitations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Submerged Solids in Opaque Liquids:} Monocular RGB and depth cannot penetrate opaque gravies or sambar to count submerged vegetable chunks; mass is estimated from surface area and recipe priors.
\item \textbf{Grain Packing Porosity:} Aerated fluffy basmati rice ($0.70\text{ g/cm}^3$) versus compressed biryani ($0.95\text{ g/cm}^3$) causes an irreducible volumetric density variance of $\pm 15\%$.
\item \textbf{Unreferenced Perspective Bias:} On captures without a reference card, phone sensor variance causes up to $5.3\times$ between-plate residual variance.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and Next Phase}
NutriSense resolves the primary failure modes of multi-item institutional meal analysis through geometric grounding, semantic vessel filtering, and precision fusion. All 380 regression tests pass, and the locked manifest is 100\% verified. The next development phase focuses on physics-informed synthetic data generation (Blender rigid-body and fluid simulation, exact mesh volume integration, and depth-locked ControlNet appearance transfer) to scale training across 10,000 augmented thali variations without manual annotation cost.
